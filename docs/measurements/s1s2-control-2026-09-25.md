# System 1 / System 2 control run: plain Pi against Pi with System 1

Recorded 2026-09-25 for **US-030** and **US-029**.
[Design](../system1-system2-harness.md) · [Pilot](s1s2-vibe-2026-09-25.md) ·
per-run aggregates: [s1s2-control-2026-09-25.json](s1s2-control-2026-09-25.json).

**Scope.** The operator's third ruling. The pilot's six harness tasks ran twice
each in two arms that differ only in System 1: *plain Pi* (C) and *Pi + System
1* (B). The model was DeepSeek V4.1 Flash, network access was limited to the
model, one upstream was fixed, and spend had a hard stop at $2 on the pilot's
existing key.

## Result

**System 1 cut cost by about 22% and System 2 turns by about 18%, and neither
cut is inside the noise.** Judged quality did not move, and hidden tests tied
on behavior. Wall-clock fell about 17%, but that change is inside the noise.
Under the operator's rule, the next step is a sized plan for a full evaluation,
[below](#sized-plan-for-a-full-evaluation-awaiting-approval), not a run.

## What System 1 did, and whether System 2 used it

The operator asked: if System 1 still barely acts, is Jev never consulted, or
consulted and ignored? The answer is neither. Jev was consulted in every run,
and System 2 used the one intervention that moved the numbers. Most of Jev's
other advice was withheld by System 1's own gates.

- **Consulted.** 96 Jev calls in 12 runs, about one for every seven System 2
  tool calls (682), and none failed. Jev spent 22 s in total (229 ms per call)
  and $0.010.
- **Brief (used).** In 10 of 12 runs, System 1 named 35 files at the start. In
  h2, no file cleared the threshold in either run. With the brief, System 2
  opened 24 of those files by turn 2; without it, plain Pi opened 13 of the
  same files that early. Pi + System 1 made its first edit earlier in 9 of 12
  paired runs, later in 1, and at a median of turn 15 against 17.5. System 2
  never opened 7 of the 35 named files, and plain Pi never opened 6.
- **Monitor (mostly withheld).** 52 checks. 16 notes reached System 2, all of
  them "narrow the search". In 21 more checks, Jev gave advice with at least
  0.6 confidence, often "verify now" at 0.98, but a periodic check speaks only
  when Jev also calls the agent stuck or off-task with at least 0.8 probability.
  The agents were rarely either, so that advice never reached System 2.
- **Triage.** 17 long outputs were judged: 8 were elided to their relevant
  lines, and 9 were left unchanged.
- **Done gate (used where it bit).** Across the settles, it chose and ran 13
  repository checks. In both h2 runs, the chosen `./scripts/verify` failed
  (exit 2); System 1 sent System 2 back with the evidence, and the rerun passed.
  Once it reminded System 2 of unverified work, and once it did nothing.

The saving follows from the brief. Reading the right files first removes early
exploration turns, and every turn carries the whole cached context, so cache
reads (−31%) and output (−24%) fall with them. [INFERENCE: no ablation isolated
the brief from the other batteries.]

## Spend

- **Scored runs.** $0.843 billed per call: plain Pi $0.467, and Pi + System 1
  $0.376 including $0.010 of Jev. Every one of the 1,036 model calls was served
  by DeepSeek's own endpoint, so the pinned upstream held.
- **Discarded.** $0.047 for a first attempt at h2 r1 in plain Pi. The spend cap
  cut it short (defect 1 below), and that task and arm were rerun.
- **Checks.** About $0.03 for check runs of the new boundary.
- **Total.** About $0.92 against the $2 hard stop. The key's usage rose $0.91
  over the same span, and the key is shared.
- **Judges.** The Jev panel cost $0.005 on the repository key. The 36 LLM
  verdicts ran on subscriptions, and all were valid.

## How the run was held to the operator's conditions

- **Network limited to the model.** Agents ran as a separate user that could
  connect only to a model boundary on loopback and to ephemeral loopback ports
  for its own test servers (`fea8212`). The boundary admitted only
  `deepseek/deepseek-v4.1-flash`, forcing upstream `deepseek` with fallbacks
  off, and the pinned Jev model. It forwarded them to an exe.dev `http-proxy`
  integration that held the key. The preflight passed on every start. Before
  the run, the agent failed to reach each of these, and the boundary refused a
  foreign model with 403:
  - github.com and raw.githubusercontent.com
  - the exe.dev gateway at 169.254.169.254, including the `llm` and `notify`
    integrations reached by name or by forced address
  - gitlab.com, a raw GitHub address, and 1.1.1.1
  - sshd, and exe.dev's Shelley agent on 127.0.0.1:9999
- **One upstream.** Every run's parity record shows the pin, and the ledger
  names DeepSeek as the upstream of all 1,036 model calls.
- **Hard stop at $2.** The boundary admitted a call only if the settled cost of
  earlier calls plus the worst case of every unsettled call fit under the cap.
  The worst case is every request byte as an uncached input token, plus the
  model's full 393,216-token completion ceiling. On the VM:
  - a $0.01 cap refused to start a run;
  - a $0.30 cap cut a run after its first call;
  - a journal holding $1.60 of unsettled calls stopped a new run at the
    dearest price window;
  - a resumed evaluation restored its earlier settled and unsettled spend.

## Defects found and fixed

1. **The cap stopped the run at $0.24 actually spent (`fea8212`).** Each call
   waited for OpenRouter's generation record. While those lagged, unsettled
   worst cases (about $0.30 each) piled up to $1.48, and the boundary refused
   the 43rd call of h2 r1 in plain Pi. Calls now settle from the `usage.cost`
   OpenRouter appends to every response (`b85f35c`). On the VM, the inline cost
   matched the generation record exactly ($0.0004869). The cut run was
   discarded and rerun.
2. **The worst case read only base prices (`fea8212`).** DeepSeek doubles its
   rates on weekdays from 01:00 to 04:00 and from 06:00 to 10:00 UTC. The run
   lasted from 20:45 to 22:21 UTC on a Friday, entirely at base prices, so every
   bound held. The bound now takes the dearest window (`b85f35c`).
3. **Resuming reset the ledger, and the spend projection miscounted
   (`e9222ce`).** Calls are now journaled when admitted and when settled, so a
   resumed evaluation counts earlier spend. The projection counts every finished
   task, resumed ones included.

The first eight runs and the capped attempt ran at `fea8212`, and the rest at
`e9222ce`. Only settlement and the projection differ between them; what the
agent saw is identical.

## Sized plan for a full evaluation (awaiting approval)

This is what the result earns under the operator's rule. Nothing below has run.

- **Question.** Does System 1's saving hold beyond one repository and one
  inexpensive model, without a quality loss? And where does Pi + System 1 stand
  against OMP (the original question)?
- **Tasks.** 24 merged pull requests: 8 small, 8 medium, and 8 large, from at
  least three repositories (harness plus two others with automated tests). A
  curator screens every hidden assertion against its statement (the h2 lesson),
  and a task whose hidden tests fail in every arm is reviewed before scoring.
- **Arms and runs.** B and C twice each per task, and OMP (A) once: 120 runs.
  An optional arm D, System 1 with only the brief, twice per task (48 more
  runs), would test the inference above that the brief carries the saving.
- **Size.** Here the task-level spread was 0.18 for cost and 0.16 for turns;
  across repositories it will be larger. At a spread of 0.3, 24 tasks detect a
  16% change in cost or turns at 80% power; at this run's spread, a 10% change.
  For judged quality, the spread of 0.29 points needs 11 tasks to detect a
  quarter-point drop, so 24 tasks cover it.
- **Model and route.** DeepSeek V4.1 Flash, as in this run, through the same
  boundary with a pinned upstream. A frontier-model check (8 tasks, B and C
  once each) is a separate decision. On OpenRouter it would cost about $5 to
  $15, and on subscriptions the runner first needs a route through the
  boundary.
- **Spend and time.** On Flash at this run's rates: about $3.40 for B and C,
  $1.20 to $2.20 for A (depending on OMP's advisor load at the pinned
  upstream), and $1.70 for D. Set the hard stop at $10. Judges run on
  subscriptions. Runs take about 3 minutes each: 6 to 8 hours on one VM, or 2
  to 3 hours split across three. Curating 24 tasks is the main effort.
- **Decision rule.** Put System 1, or the brief alone if D matches B, into the
  daily Pi profile only under three conditions:
  - the cost or turns interval for B/C lies below 1.0 across repositories;
  - judged quality is no more than a quarter point lower;
  - hidden-test pass rates are non-inferior.
  Otherwise, keep it experimental.

## Reproduce

Set up the VM and proxy as in the [pilot record](s1s2-vibe-2026-09-25.md#reproduce).
Duplicate each task in
[s1s2-vibe-2026-09-25-tasks.json](s1s2-vibe-2026-09-25-tasks.json) as `<id>-r1`
and `<id>-r2`, with matching hidden-test directories. Then run:

```sh
bun pi-config/extensions/s1s2/eval/vibe.ts --manifest tasks-replicated.json \
  --hidden <hidden> --out <out> --agent-user <agent> --code-root /opt/s1s2-src \
  --arms pi,s1s2 --model openrouter/deepseek/deepseek-v4.1-flash --thinking high \
  --max-output 384000 --upstream deepseek \
  --openrouter-base https://openrouter-eval.int.exe.xyz --spend-limit 1.97 --seed 26
```

Judge with `eval/judge.ts --arms pi,s1s2`, one process per judge
(`--judges opus`, `gemini`, `grok`, `jev`), then once without `--judges` to
aggregate.
