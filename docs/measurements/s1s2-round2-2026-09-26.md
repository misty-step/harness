# System 1 / System 2 round 2: an advisor, and one System 1 change at a time

Recorded 2026-09-26 for **US-029** and **US-030**.
[Design](../system1-system2-harness.md) · [Pilot](s1s2-vibe-2026-09-25.md) ·

**Scope.** The operator's 2026-09-26 go on the revised slate: every model on
OpenRouter with no subscription, DeepSeek V4.1 Flash as System 2, Xiaomi's
MiMo-V2.6-Pro as the advisor, the Jev-gated advisor first, the three
contestants (Pi with System 1, plain Pi, and the OMP rig) in every experiment,
and a pause before total spend passes $20.

## Calibration before any live run

Every new Jev threshold was set on round 1's recorded runs
(`eval/jev-replay.ts`), not tuned on round 2.

| Question | Replay | Threshold chosen | What it does on the replay |
|---|---|---|---|
| Advisor gate: would a review change the next step? | 1,177 turns of 30 Pi runs | 0.55 | 1.9 gate consults per run (0.7 would give 0.3) |
| Effort: is the next step routine? | same turns | 0.6 | 29% of turns run with reasoning off |
| Checklist: is a requirement still unmet? | 36 final diffs | 0.22 | about a third of runs sent back; Jev's highest unmet probability never passed 0.30 and did not track hidden failures |
| Trim: does the agent still need an old output? | 954 outputs at 83 trim points | below 0.3 trims | 59% of eligible old outputs trimmed |
| Reset: would a fresh start help? | 235 turns past 40 in 15 runs | 0.4 | 4 of 15 long runs reset, the 86-turn run among them |

A MiMo sample of ten consults on the recorded runs cost $0.028: reviews at
gate points took 10 to 19 s, final reviews 49 to 94 s, and one passed 120 s.
One reply left its advice string unterminated. The consult timeout moved to
180 s and the parser reads the two fields one by one when the JSON does not
parse.

DeepSeek V4.1 Flash on its own endpoint does not honor low or medium effort
(two low-effort calls used 443 and 2,199 reasoning tokens, two high-effort
calls 1,085 and 1,143), so the effort arm switches reasoning off rather than
lowering it.

## Decision rules, fixed before the results

Written at 20:10 UTC on 26 September, with 5 of 12 tasks through experiment 1
and nothing else run:

- **Best System 1 version for the model check (experiment 4).** Pi with
  System 1 alone, unless another System 1 arm beats it on judged quality with a
  95% interval above zero, or cuts billed cost with an interval below 1× while
  its quality interval stays above −0.25 points (round 1's quarter-point
  rule). If several qualify, the larger quality gain wins, then the lower cost.
- **Experiment 7 (hand flagged turns to MiMo).** Runs only if MiMo doing the
  work beats Pi with System 1 on judged quality with an interval above zero;
  otherwise it is skipped, as the slate specified.
- **Every other experiment** is reported against its reference with its 95%
  interval, and called a change only when that interval excludes no change.

## Experiment 1: the advisor (12 tasks, fully judged)

The reference is Pi with System 1 alone (`s1s2`). Each interval is paired
over the same 12 tasks; cost, turns, and wall-clock are geometric-mean ratios,
and quality is the mean difference in points on the 1-to-5 rubric. A ratio
below 1 means the named arm used less. The judge pass included plain Pi and
OMP as common references; all seven arms had 12 completed runs and valid
verdicts from both LLM judges and the Jev panel.

| Arm versus reference | Billed cost ratio (95% interval) | Turn ratio (95% interval) | Wall-clock ratio (95% interval) | Quality difference (95% interval) | Hidden tests passed |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| System 1 / plain Pi | 0.802 (0.646 to 0.995) | 0.768 (0.596 to 0.989) | 0.812 (0.655 to 1.008) | +0.104 (−0.282 to +0.490) | 11/12 versus 10/12 |
| OMP / plain Pi | 3.108 (2.183 to 4.424) | 1.432 (1.005 to 2.040) | 3.009 (1.858 to 4.873) | −0.135 (−0.593 to +0.322) | 11/12 versus 10/12 |
| Jev-gated advisor / System 1 | 1.479 (1.128 to 1.940) | 0.998 (0.741 to 1.343) | 2.595 (1.872 to 3.598) | +0.021 (−0.305 to +0.347) | 11/12 versus 11/12 |
| Every-turn advisor / System 1 | 1.389 (1.086 to 1.778) | 1.055 (0.807 to 1.379) | 1.460 (1.028 to 2.072) | −0.083 (−0.593 to +0.426) | 11/12 versus 11/12 |
| Advisor as a tool / System 1 | 1.108 (0.878 to 1.398) | 1.029 (0.857 to 1.235) | 1.963 (1.204 to 3.202) | +0.177 (−0.134 to +0.488) | 12/12 versus 11/12 |
| MiMo as System 2 / System 1 | 2.351 (1.790 to 3.089) | 0.913 (0.738 to 1.130) | 5.742 (4.229 to 7.795) | −0.646 (−1.201 to −0.091) | 10/12 versus 11/12 |

The advisor was actually consulted: the gated arm made 41 consults across
12 runs (11 after the first edit, 18 when Jev opened the gate, 12 before
finishing); 27 delivered advice and 14 had nothing to add. The every-turn
arm made 110 consults, delivering advice 46 times; the tool arm made 11
consults, delivering advice 9 times. No tested advisor setting demonstrated
a judged-quality improvement, while the gated and every-turn settings cost
more. These unadjusted intervals cover many related comparisons, so a
just-below-one cost interval is a lead, not replication. OMP did not recover
its round-1 pilot's judged edge in this matched model setup; that weakens,
but does not isolate or disprove, the earlier advisor explanation.

**Experiment 7 is skipped under the pre-set rule:** MiMo as System 2 did
not beat System 1 on judged quality; its interval was entirely below zero.
The preliminary two-attempt, trim, and reset smoke runs also completed:
best-of-two made two independent attempts and Jev picked one (both passed
repository checks); trim replaced five old outputs; reset fired once with
an advisor handoff. All three passed their withheld tests and regression
checks. They prove the actions occur, not that they improve quality.

## Experiments 2, 3, and 5: one change at a time (12 tasks, fully judged)

These arms use the same DeepSeek setup and the same 12 tasks as experiment 1,
but a separate blind judge pass that includes Pi, OMP, and unmodified System 1
as references. Quality values are meaningful **within** a pass; a score from
one pass is not compared with a score from another.

| Change versus unmodified System 1 | Billed cost ratio (95% interval) | Turn ratio (95% interval) | Wall-clock ratio (95% interval) | Quality difference (95% interval) | Hidden tests passed |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Requirement checklist | 1.074 (0.883 to 1.307) | 1.079 (0.870 to 1.338) | 1.120 (0.912 to 1.377) | −0.177 (−0.586 to +0.231) | 10/12 versus 11/12 |
| Reasoning off when Jev says routine | 0.695 (0.468 to 1.032) | 0.607 (0.427 to 0.862) | 0.606 (0.409 to 0.897) | −0.875 (−1.716 to −0.034) | 8/12 versus 11/12 |
| Richer brief (tests and definitions) | 1.055 (0.874 to 1.275) | 1.133 (0.927 to 1.385) | 1.125 (0.935 to 1.353) | −0.083 (−0.450 to +0.283) | 11/12 versus 11/12 |
| Brief alone (without triage, monitor, or done gate) | 1.006 (0.797 to 1.269) | 1.026 (0.761 to 1.384) | 1.033 (0.808 to 1.322) | +0.042 (−0.242 to +0.326) | 11/12 versus 11/12 |

The checklist sent System 2 back on 4 of 12 tasks; it did not show a gain.
Jev chose reasoning off on 39 turns and on for 145, with one fail-open.
That arm used fewer turns and less time, but its judged quality fell and
three more hidden tests failed. The cost interval still includes 1. The
richer brief and brief-alone arm did not separate from unmodified System 1
on the paired measures. The many unadjusted comparisons make the
just-below-zero quality interval for reasoning-off a warning, not proof of
a general effect; the coincident hidden-test losses strengthen the case
against adopting it on this evidence.

Three plain-Pi tasks were repeated after this batch to measure drift.
The root-mean-square log of repeat/first-run ratios was 0.238 for billed
cost, 0.216 for turns, and 0.251 for wall-clock. These are only three
tasks, not a precise population noise estimate.

## Experiments 6, 8, and 9: context and repeated attempts (12 tasks, fully judged)

The same 12 tasks and unmodified System 1 reference were judged in a third
blind pass with Pi and OMP included. These are paired, unadjusted 95%
intervals; quality differences compare candidates only within this pass.

| Change versus unmodified System 1 | Billed cost ratio (95% interval) | Turn ratio (95% interval) | Wall-clock ratio (95% interval) | Quality difference (95% interval) | Hidden tests passed |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Trim old outputs | 1.214 (0.964 to 1.529) | 0.994 (0.796 to 1.243) | 1.023 (0.826 to 1.267) | −0.104 (−0.350 to +0.142) | 11/12 versus 11/12 |
| Fresh start when stuck | 1.128 (0.959 to 1.326) | 1.107 (0.875 to 1.400) | 1.195 (0.994 to 1.437) | −0.198 (−0.525 to +0.129) | 11/12 versus 11/12 |

Trim replaced 26 old outputs; reset occurred only once, with a handoff.
Both ran, but neither improved the paired measures. Best-of-two made two
independent attempts on every task. The repository checks passed for both
attempts on all 12, so Jev chose every time (the second attempt four times).
Its small judged-quality lead has an interval crossing zero, and its chosen
changes passed fewer hidden tests while consuming roughly twice the cost
and turns. The check-based pick did not separate candidates in this slate.

**Model-check choice under the pre-set rule:** unmodified Pi with System 1.
None of the other System 1 arms had a quality interval entirely above zero
against it, nor a cost interval entirely below 1 while keeping the quality
lower bound above −0.25 points. The nearest quality lead, best-of-two at
+0.177 points (−0.030 to +0.384), cost 1.982 times as much. Experiment 4
used the unmodified arm against plain Pi and OMP on the three other main
models.

## Experiment 4: three other models (six tasks each; Qwen confounded)

The pre-selected, unmodified System 1 arm ran against plain Pi and the OMP
rig on GLM-5.3-Flash, MiMo-V2.6-Flash, and Qwen3.8-Flash. Each model used
the same six round-one tasks (`h37`, `h57`, `h21`, `h26`, `h2`, `h43`).
All 54 processes exited with code zero, had at least one parent turn, and
avoided timeouts; exit zero does **not** mean the task was completed. Both
blind LLM judges and the Jev panel returned valid verdicts for all six
tasks per model. The intervals below pair contestants by task within a
model, unadjusted, with only six observations. Qwen's pinned Alibaba
upstream returned **133 HTTP 429s across 16 of its 18 runs**, including
every arm on `h37`, `h21`, and `h26`. Its rows are observed outcomes under
throttling, **not** a clean estimate of harness effects or transferable
cost savings.

System 1's cheaper runs repeated on MiMo; GLM's interval is too wide to
establish a cost difference. The MiMo quality interval still permits a
substantial gain or loss. OMP cost about 2.9 times Pi on GLM and 4.9 times
Pi on MiMo, with no demonstrated judged-quality advantage. Six tasks from
one repository, multiple unadjusted comparisons, and different provider
behavior preclude a general transfer claim.

On Qwen, System 1 passed one hidden suite of six versus four for plain Pi
and five for OMP. Its `h37`, `h21`, and `h26` changes were empty: the model
identified relevant work and then repeatedly received upstream 429s,
ending after 8, 23, and 13 turns, while the passing plain-Pi runs took
52, 58, and 42. All three arms were throttled on these tasks, so this
does not isolate System 1 as the cause. On `h43`, System 1 implemented the
inventory script but omitted the task's session-close message and OMP
settings; its initial brief picked no files, and the upstream also
throttled it. This may involve context drift as well as provider failure.
The cheap Qwen cost ratio is not a quality-preserving saving.

The next informative experiment is two fresh, paired Qwen seeds for plain
Pi and unmodified System 1 on all six tasks, only when the provider is
stable. Invalidate a pair with sustained 429s instead of grading an
interrupted no-patch run as a completed solution; judge both arms blind
and check hidden tests. At the observed $0.543 run cost for the two arms
plus about $0.149 judging per six-task pass, two repeats suggest $1.38;
use a $2 cap if separately authorized. This is a recommendation, not an
automatic extension of the slate.
